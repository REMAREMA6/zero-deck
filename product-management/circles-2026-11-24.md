# CIRCLES working record — MVP product design (2026-11-24)

Go received 2026-11-24. Method: the rulebook's seven skills, worked in order. This file is the
reasoning record; the owner-facing document is generated from it after this step.

## C — Comprehend

- **Goal (fixed):** the MVP exists to earn and keep user trust by giving an honest verdict with a
  safe next step at the moment of suspicion. Not engagement, not revenue for v1. This is forced by
  the binding honest-results doctrine (recon §2.1): the product's differentiator IS honesty.
- **Metric the goal implies:** return-when-it-matters behaviour — repeat scans within 14 days
  (full definition in S below). Guardrail metric: zero states in the build that claim 100% safety.
- **Greenfield or improvement:** greenfield build inside a fixed frame — 18 screens specified and
  prototyped, four-verdict set fixed (Threat found / Needs your review / No known threat found /
  Could not decide), Intelligent Hybrid Quarantine policy fixed (recon §2.1, §10).
- **Constraints:** solo builder; no lab; no file content ever leaves the phone; backend is one
  small Next.js service; GSB quota unverified; feed commercial terms unverified; engine licensing
  (Option A/B) open — and deliberately out of scope here because it is an engine-path choice, not a
  product-experience choice (either option produces the same four verdicts).
- **Context (5W1H):** What — Android scanner app + admin web panel. Who — phone users who receive
  suspicious files/links in chats, email, or their Downloads folder. Why — the alternatives all
  cost trust or privacy: Play Protect gives opaque verdicts, online scanners demand uploading the
  private file, big AVs are paid and noisy. When — verdicts needed in seconds, at the moment of
  hesitation. Where — on the device; admin plane secondary. How today — nothing shipped; the
  prototype screens exist but carry no filled verdict states yet.
- **Assumptions, named:** (a1) the 18-screen design and four-verdict set stand — confirmed against
  recon §2.1; (a2) the target user will not buy a paid AV in this window; (a3) product design is
  engine-agnostic (A/B decision does not change verdicts or screens — it changes only the Kotlin
  module's origin); (a4) success measurement uses app-local/backend aggregates, no PII.

## I — Identify

- **Cohorts:** (1) everyday Android user who just received a suspicious file or link in a chat,
  email, or download, and hesitates before opening it — friction: no trustworthy, private way to
  check; every existing path costs privacy (upload the file) or trust (opaque verdicts).
  (2) power user / junior analyst who wants the evidence behind a verdict — friction: consumer
  tools hide it. (3) operator — admin-side; friction: cannot see verdict health or feed freshness.
- **Chosen cohort: (1).** Largest affected group; the friction is most acute at exactly the moment
  the product exists for; the honest-results doctrine is written for them — an analyst tolerates
  "Could not decide", an everyday user is harmed by unexplained uncertainty. Analyst depth is
  served later via evidence views, not as the design target.
- **Other side, acknowledged:** the admin/operator side exists (admin sign-in, dashboard, user
  management, threat information, help content, audit logs — six of the eighteen screens). The MVP
  experience is designed for the user side; the admin plane ships as minimum operational tooling,
  which is a stated choice, not a silent one.
- **Stakeholders:** the owner (builder-operator), Google (GSB terms), the feed providers
  (licences), and the users whose trust is the product's only asset.

## R — Report

The feature list is the want. The needs are what the features are for. All needs below are marked
reported (comes from the owner's fixed policies) or inferred (reconstructed from the doctrine and
detection limits — no user research exists yet).

| Want on the list | Need behind it (the job) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| File + link scanner, scan result screens | N1 — Trust the verdict: "when I am about to open something that might hurt me, I need a verdict that says exactly what the app knows and does not know, without my file leaving my phone, so I can act with clear eyes." | Inferred from the doctrine + hash-detection limits (recon §5.4: hash matching cannot clear unknown files — "Could not decide" is structurally necessary, not decorative) |
| Quarantine screens | N2 — Contain the threat: "when the verdict is dangerous, I need the threat neutralized by the app, not just announced, so I am not left holding it." | Reported (Intelligent Hybrid Quarantine policy is fixed) |
| History + restore flows | N3 — Recover and audit: "I need to see what the app did and why, before and after, so trust survives even when the app is wrong." | Reported (restore-only-after-fresh-scan policy is fixed) |
| Admin dashboard, audit logs | N4 — Operate honestly: "as the operator, I need verdict health and feed freshness visible, so honesty stays true after launch." | Inferred (audit-log screens exist) |
| Help screens, Sanity content | N5 — Know what to do next: "when the app says something scary, I need plain next steps, so fear does not freeze me." | Inferred |

User stories (outcome clause carries the need, not the feature):
- N1: As an Android user who just received a file from a group chat, I want to know whether it is
  known-dangerous, known-clean-or-unmatched, or undetermined — and on what evidence — so that I can
  act without blind trust or blind fear.
- N2: As a user whose file was confirmed malicious, I want the app to lock it away immediately, so
  that I cannot open it by accident and do not need to know how to handle malware.
- N3: As a user, I want my scans and quarantined items visible and reversible, so that a mistake by
  the app never costs me my files.
- N4: As the operator, I want verdict and feed health on one screen, so that I can keep the
  app's promises true after launch.
- N5: As a frightened user holding a "Threat found" card, I want plain next steps, so that I act
  instead of freezing.

## C — Cut

Axes (named): frequency of the pain, acuteness, alignment with the trust goal. Light RICE as
input (reach = first-quarter install cohort, assumed 1,000 users; marked as an assumption, not
data; confidence values honest because no research exists).

| Need | Freq | Acuteness | Goal alignment | RICE (assumed) | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N1 trust the verdict | Every scan | High | Total | R 1000 × I 3 × C 80% ÷ E 1 = 2,400 | Wins — the chosen need |
| N2 contain the threat | Only on a hit | Highest | High | R 50 × I 3 × C 80% ÷ E 1.5 = 80 | Ships anyway — stated override |
| N3 recover and audit | Moderate | Moderate | High | R 400 × I 2 × C 80% ÷ E 0.5 = 1,280 | Keep, minimal |
| N4 operate honestly | Operator-only | Moderate | Operational | R 1 × I 2 × C 100% ÷ E 1 = 200 | Minimum operational tooling |
| N5 know what to do next | At the verdict | High | High | folded into N1 | Fold into verdict cards |

**The override, stated out loud (rulebook's legitimate override reason: dependency):** N2's score
is small because hits are rare — but N1's promise is broken if a "Threat found" card has no safe
action behind it. N2 is a dependency of N1, not a competitor. Same for N5: a frightening verdict
without next steps freezes the user at the exact moment the product exists for.

**What is cut, by name:** real-time background protection (later release, per design doc);
browser-extension scanning (later release); admin analytics and dashboard polish (v1.1+);
standalone help-site build-out (Sanity ships minimal, verdict-embedded guidance first); onboarding
beyond the already-designed welcome screens. **Refused on principle, not deferred:** a security
score or "protection level" widget — a score manufactured from absence of findings is precisely
the "nothing found is not safe" lie the doctrine forbids.

## L — List

Chosen need: N1 — the verdict experience. Three options that differ in kind (single moment →
closed loop → ambient protection), not three skins of one screen:

- **Option 1 — Quick win: verdict-only scanner.** One scan surface (file + link), the four verdict
  cards with evidence lines and honest copy, plain history list. Dangerous files get advice only
  (delete it yourself); no quarantine UI. Ships fastest.
- **Option 2 — Core bet: verdict + containment loop.** Everything in Option 1, plus quarantine as
  a first-class user-facing state (list, details, restore-after-rescan), verdict-specific actions
  wired into the cards, and history doubling as the audit trail with a per-record evidence view.
  Maps one-to-one onto the already-designed 18 screens — no new screens, states filled in.
- **Option 3 — Horizon: ambient guardian.** Always-on monitoring (download interception,
  file-observer pattern), proactive alerts, deep-scan integration. Named to show the true shape of
  the problem; explicitly not the MVP.

Discarded variations (generated past the three, kept as evidence of breadth): verdict-as-chat
(declined — adds a habit), gamified streaks (declined — conflicts with doctrine), social
verdict-sharing (declined — privacy cost).

## E — Evaluate

Axes fixed before reading the options: trust delivered (does the loop close), viable (solo-
buildable AND reusable — people come back), lovable (fits the moment of hesitation, no new habit),
cost/time to ship, visible-failure risk (who absorbs a wrong answer).

| Axis | Opt 1 verdict-only | Opt 2 verdict + loop | Opt 3 ambient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trust delivered | Half — announces danger, strands the user | Full — every verdict ends in a safe action + record | Full but claims more than it can prove |
| Viable (repeat use) | One-shot utility | History + containment give return reasons | Highest, but needs the install base first |
| Lovable | Fits the moment | Fits the moment | Adds a habit (ambient vigilance) |
| Cost/time | Weeks | Weeks + quarantine states | Months, new infra |
| Visible-failure risk | False "no threat" anxiety | False-positive quarantine locks a good file (mitigated: restore-after-rescan + evidence shown) | Biggest blast radius; background-service failures on-device |

- **Drawbacks of the favoured option (Option 2), written down:** largest state surface to get
  right (encrypted store, restore gating, Android 13+ storage-access quirks); weeks slower than
  Option 1; quarantine edge cases will surface publicly. Acceptance: bounded by the cuts already
  made in C (admin minimal, help folded in).
- **Who absorbs a wrong answer:** false positive → user's anxiety and one locked file (recoverable
  by design); false negative → the user, unrecoverably — mitigated by honest copy ("No known
  threat found" is not a clean bill) and never claiming safety. Bloom-filter probabilistic
  false positives → rescan flow, per recon §9.
- **Probabilistic-output check (rulebook's AI-era trade-offs):** verdicts are deterministic
  (hash match) but Bloom filters are probabilistic; "Could not decide" is the system refusing to
  offload unexplained uncertainty onto the user — the editability/absorbability design already
  exists in the verdict set.

## S — Summarize

- **One recommendation:** ship the MVP as **Option 2 — verdict + containment loop**: scan-first
  home, four honest verdict cards with evidence and next-step actions, quarantine as first-class
  containment with restore-after-rescan, history as the audit trail, admin reduced to minimum
  operational tooling, help folded into verdict screens. It is the only option in which every
  verdict ends in a safe action and a visible record — the trust loop closes, which is the goal.
- **One testable metric:** the 14-day return-scan rate — of users who complete a first scan, the
  share who run another scan or open their scan history within 14 days, against a pre-set target
  of 25%. The target is chosen before launch (so it cannot be moved after seeing data) and marked
  as a chosen threshold, not an industry benchmark; it will be recalibrated with first-cohort
  data. It distinguishes "it worked" from "people tried it once": one-scan-only is novelty;
  returning is the tool earning a place.
- **The trade-off, named:** choosing Option 2 gives up Option 1's earlier ship date (weeks of
  quarantine-state work) and defers Option 3's ambient protection. What is bought: the promise is
  fully kept at the moment it matters most — the moment of a "Threat found" verdict.
- **Anticipated objections:** "quarantine is scope creep" — it is the already-fixed IQH policy
  made tangible, and was always a build item; "ship Option 1 and add the loop later" — the
  stranded-at-'Threat found' moment is exactly when trust dies, and retrofitting re-ships the same
  screens; "why 25%?" — a pre-set threshold makes the metric a test instead of a hope.
- **What I do not know:** the needs are inferred (no user interviews yet); GSB quota figures;
  per-feed commercial rights; the Option A/B licensing decision (engine path, not product
  design). All four are flagged, not assumed.
- **Status:** this is a recommendation with its reasoning and sources. Where it lands is the
  owner's call.
