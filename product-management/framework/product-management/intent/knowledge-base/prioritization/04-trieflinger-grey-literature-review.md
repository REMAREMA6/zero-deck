## What the paper is (from the abstract)

- **Context:** product success depends on identifying *what* to launch and *in what order* to deliver the most value to customer and business; most companies prioritize roadmap items based on opinion of experts or management; market dynamics, rapidly evolving technologies, and changing customer behavior complicate prioritization.
- **Objective:** identify suitable techniques for prioritizing a product roadmap in dynamic and uncertain market environments.
- **Method:** a **Grey Literature Review** following the guidelines of Garousi et al. (2019) — i.e. includes practitioner/professional sources, not just academic ones.
- **Results:**
  - **18 techniques** for product roadmap prioritization identified.
  - **15 techniques** are primarily used to prioritize *outputs* by considering factors such as expected impact or effort.
  - **2 techniques** are most suitable for prioritizing *risky assumptions* that need to be validated.
  - **1 technique** focuses on the prioritization of *outcomes*.
  - All techniques have in common: they should be conducted as a **cross-functional team activity** to include different perspectives.

## Why it matters and where it sits in the literature

- This is one of the few pieces that explicitly catalogs practitioner-facing roadmap-prioritization techniques at the "everything feels important" stage — the situation where raw demand exceeds capacity and the roadmap has to be cut.
- Its 18-technique count is the "grey literature" figure often cited alongside the academic SLR figure (Achimugu et al. 2014: ~49 requirement-prioritization methodologies — see prioritization/03).
- Sibling grey-literature works by the same group: "Product Roadmapping Processes for an Uncertain Market Environment: A Grey Literature Review" (LNBIP 2021) and "Product Roadmap Formats for an Uncertain Future" (SEAA 2020) — collectively an "uncertain market environment" research stream.
