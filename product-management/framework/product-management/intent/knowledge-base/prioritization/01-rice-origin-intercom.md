# RICE: Simple prioritization for product managers

**Source:** Intercom Blog — the original RICE article
**URL:** https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/
**Author:** Sean McBride (Intercom), published January 5, 2018 (original 2016)
**Fetched:** 2026-09-30

Note: This is the origin article for the RICE framework (Sean McBride at Intercom). The RICE score measures "total impact per time worked".

## Why prioritization is difficult

- It's satisfying to work on pet ideas you'd use yourself, instead of projects with broad reach.
- It's tempting to focus on clever ideas, instead of projects that directly impact your goals.
- It's exciting to dive into new ideas, instead of projects you're already confident about.
- It's easy to discount the additional effort one project will require over another.

## The four factors

RICE = Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort.

### Reach
Estimate how many people each project will affect within a given period (e.g. "how many customers will this project impact over a single quarter?"). Measured in number of people/events per time period (customers per quarter, transactions per month). Use real measurements from product metrics where possible.

Example: Project 1 has 500 customers reaching a point in the signup funnel each month, 30% choose the option → reach = 500 × 30% × 3 = 450 customers per quarter. Project 2: every customer using this feature per quarter sees the change → 2,000 customers per quarter. Project 3: one-time effect on 800 existing customers → 800 per quarter.

### Impact
Estimate the impact on an individual person ("how much will this increase conversion rate when a customer encounters it?"). Multiple-choice scale: 3 = massive, 2 = high, 1 = medium, 0.5 = low, 0.25 = minimal. "Choosing an impact number may seem unscientific. But remember the alternative: a tangled mess of gut feeling."

### Confidence
Factor in your level of confidence about your estimates ("how much support do you really have?"). Scale: 100% = high confidence, 80% = medium, 50% = low, anything below = "total moonshot".

### Effort
Estimate total time from all team members (product, design, engineering) as "person-months". Keep estimates rough — whole numbers (or 0.5 for anything well under a month). More effort is bad, so it divides the total impact.

## The formula

Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort

The resulting score measures "total impact per time worked". Intercom published a spreadsheet template with the article.

After initial scoring: sort, re-evaluate. Are there projects where the score seems too high or too low? Reconsider estimates, or accept that your gut may be wrong.

## Using RICE effectively

RICE scores shouldn't be a hard and fast rule. Reasons to work on a lower-scored project first: dependency for another project; "table stakes" to sell to certain customers. With a scoring system in place you can clearly identify when you're making these trade-offs — and defend decisions to others.
