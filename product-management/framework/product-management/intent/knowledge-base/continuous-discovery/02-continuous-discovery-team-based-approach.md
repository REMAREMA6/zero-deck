# Getting to a team-based approach to continuous discovery

**Source:** Mind the Product — summary of Teresa Torres's #mtpcon Digital APAC keynote
**URL:** https://www.mindtheproduct.com/getting-to-a-team-based-approach-to-continuous-discovery-by-teresa-torres/
**Author:** Eira Hayward (summary of keynote by Teresa Torres), April 16, 2021
**Fetched:** 2026-09-30

## Discovery vs delivery

Product discovery is deciding what to build; product delivery is the building and scaling of a production-quality product.

## The problem: project mindset

Over the last 10 years discovery practices evolved rapidly — more teams interviewing customers, running experiments, testing assumptions, running usability tests, doing rapid prototyping. But lots of teams still take a project approach: initial research, then rely on that research for the rest of the project. The world keeps changing, so continuous discovery is better. Many teams think they're doing continuous discovery but approach it with a project mindset.

## What does continuous discovery mean? (Teresa's definition)

At a minimum:
- **weekly touchpoints with customers**
- **by the team building the product**
- where that team is conducting **small research activities**
- in pursuit of a **desired product outcome**.

### Weekly touchpoints

We make product decisions every day — all would benefit from customer input. Weekly input avoids a validation mindset, catches early feedback, and enables a co-creation mindset. Adopt a continuous improvement mindset — incremental improvements each week. "Think of this as a benchmark to aspire to. I've worked with hundreds of teams that have successfully found this cadence."

### The team building the product

The product trio — product manager, design lead, tech lead working together (vs older waterfall models). Best teams work as Marty Cagan's "missionaries." Teresa gets pushback on how the trio should work; she suggests discovery starts with the trio to move quickly and get quick decisions. Others (data analysts, user researchers, product marketers) can be involved, but "the trio can flex, but know that you'll trade inclusivity with speed."

### Small research activities

Key to unlocking a continuous discovery cadence — like Kanban's limiting WIP and Agile's small batches.

### Desired product outcomes

Teams can forget their job is to deliver value to the customer. Research must serve driving the product outcome, because the product outcome creates customer value in a way that also creates business value.

## The Opportunity Solution Tree (recap)

Business outcomes tend to be financial metrics; product outcomes tend to be measures of customer behavior.

- **Define a clear desired outcome.** Netflix example: business outcome = gross subscriber revenue; product outcome = something the team can influence (engagement metrics, shows watched) to drive that business goal. Clarity matters because it's where the business negotiates with Product about where to focus, and the team translates it into a product outcome appropriate to their span of work.
- **Discover opportunities to drive the outcome.** An opportunity = customer needs, pain points, desires. Resolves the tension between business needs and customer needs (e.g., online newspapers forcing readers through ads): "If instead we start with the business need and ask what are the customer needs that could drive that business need, we reconcile that tension immediately, and we still stay customer centric."
- **Discover solutions** that deliver on those opportunities. Most teams are good at solution discovery (A/B testing, lean startup, usability testing).

## Continuous interviewing

Interviewing should be continuous and generative — collect customer stories and discover opportunities rather than solutions.

- **Recruiting is the biggest barrier.** Automate recruitment: (1) recruit people who visit your site; (2) define triggers for support teams (customer-facing teams recruit); (3) customer advisory boards for hard-to-recruit audiences.
- **Ask the right questions:** avoid speculation; focus on collecting stories; listen for opportunities to map the opportunity space.
- **Work on multiple solutions for the same opportunity** — more people involved, more diverse ideas. Working on one idea at a time sets you up for confirmation bias. "You want to test sets of solutions that target the same opportunity and look for a clear frontrunner."
- **Break solutions into underlying assumptions and test rapidly.** Ideas rest on assumptions that things are desirable, viable, feasible, ethical and usable. "The more diverse your team is, the more likely it is you'll catch your riskiest assumptions — this is definitely an area where you want the whole team involved."
