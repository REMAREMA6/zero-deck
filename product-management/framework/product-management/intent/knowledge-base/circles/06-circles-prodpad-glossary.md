## What it is

- The CIRCLES method helps Product Managers break down and tackle product questions and challenges; a guide for thinking about everything important when making changes to a product.
- First introduced as a device for answering **product sense** interview questions; since broadened into a general problem-solving framework in Product Management (designing new features, resolving user pain points, improving workflows).
- CIRCLES is an acronym for the seven stages (ProdPad's own wording, with the mnemonic emoji):
  1. **Comprehend the situation** 🤔 – understand the problem space
  2. **Identify the customer** 🔎 – pinpoint who you're designing for
  3. **Report the customer's needs** 🗣️ – articulate their pain points
  4. **Cut with prioritization** ✂️ – focus on what matters most
  5. **List solutions** 💡 – brainstorm possibilities
  6. **Evaluate tradeoffs** ⚖️ – weigh options thoughtfully
  7. **Summarize recommendations** ✍️ – deliver a clear path forward

## Step-by-step guidance (ProdPad's reading)

1. **Comprehend the situation** – get the facts before solving; ask qualifying questions (which metrics matter, which product areas, what customers like); use the **5 W's and H** — What is it? Who is it for? Why do they need it? When is it available? Where is it available? How does it work? Then consider constraints: resources, budget, timescale. ("You can and SHOULD ask what a, b, or c is.")
2. **Identify the customer** – know the audience before building (dinner-party analogy: don't cook beef Wellington for vegans). Do this throughout the product lifecycle, not just at the start. Use user personas, user profiling, usage data, quantitative/qualitative data, product research.
3. **Report customer needs** – shape raw research into an understandable narrative. Best done with **user stories**: "As a **[type of user]**, I want **[an action]** so that **[benefit or value]**." User stories force goals/outcomes thinking instead of features.
4. **Cut (prioritize needs)** – you can't do everything; validate ideas and prune. Embedded prioritization frameworks named by ProdPad:
   - **MoSCoW** – Must-Have / Should-Have / Could-Have / Won't-Have
   - **Weighted impact scoring** – criteria (customer demand, cost, technical feasibility) × weights → total rank
   - **RICE scoring** – Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort; prioritize high-impact, low-effort initiatives
   - "Killing your darlings is just as important as finding good ones."
5. **List solutions** – be creative; aim for **at least 3 ideas** – the first idea is rarely the best; iterate; collaborate with the product team (share roadmap visibility, strategy, vision, North Star metric).
6. **Evaluate trade-offs** – no solution is perfect; find the solution with the fewest/least impactful drawbacks. Tools: **trade-off matrix** (cost, complexity, time to implement, potential user impact), **risk vs reward**, simple **pros vs cons**.
7. **Summarize recommendations** – synthesise and present a clear, actionable course of action; confident and data-backed; essentially a **30-second elevator pitch**. In an interview: (a) recommend the product/feature, (b) summarize what it is + benefits for user and company, (c) justify why over alternatives. Anticipate objections and be ready with evidence-based responses.

## Why PMs should use it

- **User-centric focus** – users at the center of the thought process
- **Effective prioritization** – cut ideas that don't work or are time sinks; plug other prioritization frameworks into the model
- **Holistic problem solving** – constraints are considered from the start, preventing infeasible "pie-in-the-sky" ideas
- **Articulate your thought process** – shows stakeholders the "why" behind a recommendation
- **Boosts your confidence** – structured approach, every critical factor considered

## Notes / cautions

- ProdPad claims CIRCLES was "introduced in 2017" — the origin is credited to **Lewis C. Lin** (interview prep: *The Product Manager Interview* and *Decode and Conquer*); other sources date the method to Lin's material well before 2017, so treat the 2017 date as best-effort, not authoritative.
- This is a glossary treatment (definition + how-to + benefits), not a worked example; deeper worked examples are in the lewis-lin, decode-and-conquer, productinterview, logrocket, and productschool files in this folder.
