# A Guide to the CIRCLES Framework with Product Examples

**Source:** Product School (third-party breakdown, supplementary)
**URL:** https://productschool.com/blog/skills/circles-framework-guide
**Author:** Carlos Gonzalez de Villaumbrosia, CEO at Product School, April 04, 2024
**Fetched:** 2026-09-30

The CIRCLES method is considered one of the top product management frameworks. It is a tour de force with all the key considerations product managers need to consider before developing and designing new products. Developed by Lewis Lin, author of Decode and Conquer.

## Key Components of the CIRCLES Framework in Product Management

### C - Comprehend the Situation

Lewis Lin recommends comprehending the situation by: (1) Clarifying the goal — is a product update meant to increase revenue or stickiness? Once you know the goal, you can decide which metrics to measure success by (e.g. if engagement is the goal, time spent on site might be a good metric). (2) Understand constraints. (3) Understand the context — what is it? Who is it for? Why do people use the product?

Example: Google Assistant — Google analyzed the broader context: increasing integration of technology in daily life, need for seamless user experiences, competitive offerings (Apple Siri, Amazon Alexa).

### I - Identify the Customer

Determining the product's target audience. Involves defining who the product is for, understanding characteristics, behaviors, and pain points.

Example: Netflix audience segmentation — by analyzing viewing habits, demographic data, preference patterns, Netflix categorizes users into distinct segments for personalized recommendations.

### R - Report Customer Needs

Outlining the target audience's specific needs: gathering and listing the essential requirements and desires of the user base. Creating use cases that align with each user persona.

Example: Spotify personalized music discovery — Spotify understood that people need help finding new favorites, and was in a unique position to offer suggestions based on personal data in a way record labels never could.

### C - Cut (via prioritization)

"you can do anything, but not everything." Prioritize needs based on feasibility, impact, and alignment with business goals. Ensures the most critical and impactful features are developed first.

Example: Intercom & the RICE method — Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort. Intercom's AI Chatbot: wide reach, high impact, high confidence, effort didn't outweigh benefits.

### L - List Solutions

Brainstorming potential features or solutions. Lin suggests coming up with at least 10 ideas — the more ideas, the more likely you land on a great one.

Example: Amazon Alexa — integrating voice assistant into household devices required a wide ecosystem; imagine how long Amazon PMs' list of solutions had to have been.

### E - Evaluate Trade-offs

Weighing benefits against drawbacks: cost, implementation complexity, potential impact on user experience. No solution is without trade-offs; evaluate whether drawbacks imply an irresponsible level of risk or work against the business strategy.

Example: Facebook News Feed algorithm adjustments — prioritizing friends/family over businesses traded reduced reach for businesses for quality of interactions; Meta decided it aligned with business objectives.

### S - Summarize Recommendations

Synthesizing insights into a recommendation that aligns with user needs, business goals, and feasibility assessments. Presenting a coherent and justified recommendation.

Example: Microsoft Windows 10 updates — highlight security enhancements, improved UX, new functionalities inspired by user feedback.

## Applying CIRCLES to an AI-Powered Chatbot (worked example)

1. **Comprehend**: clarify goal (cut costs, enhance productivity, customer delight), identify success metrics (e.g. escalation rate), understand LLM requirements and constraints.
2. **Identify**: e-commerce platforms and boutique shops — primary customers seeking to enhance customer service and streamline operations.
3. **Report**: round-the-clock service across time zones, handling common queries to reduce wait times, shopping assistance.
4. **Cut**: prioritize speed of responses, ease of integration, scalability; other features stay in backlog.
5. **List**: NLP to interpret queries, e-commerce platform compatibility, RAG incorporating the entire product inventory.
6. **Evaluate**: advanced AI capabilities require significant resources; balance advanced functionality against development time, cost, system complexity.
7. **Summarize**: develop a scalable, user-friendly AI chatbot emphasizing quick accurate responses, easy integration, personalized shopping experiences.

"They say circles are the strongest shape, and the CIRCLES method's greatest strength lies in its well-roundedness. It takes a holistic view of a product's journey from the context that brings it into existence to the final tweaks that will make it a success."
